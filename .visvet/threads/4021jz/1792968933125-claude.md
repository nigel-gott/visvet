Yes, and it does too much in one place. Line 1 is where tree-sitter runs: `extract(file, text)` parses the file. After that, everything else in it is pairing and diffing.

What it gets: `file` is the path (a plain `&str`, should be a `RepoPath`), and `new`/`old` are the file's raw bytes now and at the base, either of which may be missing (added or deleted). Bytes, because a file may not be text, e.g. an image.

What it does, in one 50-line function:

1. **Parse** both versions with tree-sitter (skipping the old one if the bytes are the same).
2. **Pair** old and new types by name, then their members by name.
3. **Diff** each pair and work out its status (added, changed…).
4. **Build** the `CodeUnit`, plus the names it mentions, which `link` turns into arrows later.

A cleaner shape: `file_objects` only calls four small functions, `parse`, `pair`, `member` and `unit`, and takes a `RepoPath`. No behaviour change.
